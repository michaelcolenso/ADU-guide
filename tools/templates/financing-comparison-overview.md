# Financing Comparison Matrix (Foundation Overview)

Use this summary for first-pass screening before lender conversations.

| Option | Typical Rate Pattern | Typical Term | Typical Max Amount | Qualification Snapshot | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HELOC | Variable, often prime-based | 10-year draw + 10-20 year repayment | Based on available equity and CLTV limits | Strong equity position, acceptable DTI, credit profile | Flexible draws, pay interest only on what you use during draw | Variable rate risk, payment shock after draw period |
| Home Equity Loan | Fixed | 5-30 years | Based on available equity and CLTV limits | Similar to HELOC, often stronger payment history expectations | Predictable payment, fixed rate | Less flexible than HELOC once funded |
| Cash-Out Refinance | Fixed or adjustable | Commonly 15 or 30 years | Based on appraised value and lender LTV limits | Re-qualification on full mortgage profile | Can simplify into one loan, may reduce blended rate in favorable markets | Resets first mortgage terms and closing costs |
| Construction Loan | Variable during construction, converts or refinances later | 6-18 month build period (+ permanent financing term) | Based on as-completed value, borrower profile, and draw schedule | Detailed plans, licensed contractor, contingency, stronger documentation | Built for phased draws and construction workflow | More complex underwriting and admin burden |
| Fannie Mae HomeStyle | Market mortgage rates | Long-term mortgage term | Program and lender limits apply | Primary residence focus, renovation scope documentation | Can finance construction within conforming framework | More documentation, lender overlays vary |
| FHA 203(k) | FHA-linked rates | Long-term mortgage term | Program and FHA county limits apply | Owner-occupant requirements, consultant/document steps | Lower down payment potential vs some conventional products | Process complexity and program constraints |
| Cash / Family Capital | No lender-set rate | Flexible | Available liquidity only | Depends on personal/family balance sheet | Fastest execution and fewer underwriting constraints | Concentrates risk and can reduce reserves |
| State / Local ADU Programs | Program-specific | Program-specific | Program caps apply | Jurisdiction-specific eligibility and income/property criteria | Grants/subsidies can improve project viability | Limited availability, often narrow eligibility windows |

## Notes for Use

- Confirm current rates, fees, and underwriting standards with licensed lenders before selecting an option.
- For any financed option, model at least three scenarios: baseline, +10% project cost, and delayed completion.
- Keep contingency outside your minimum required draw budget whenever possible.

## Suggested Inputs for the Interactive Version

- Home value
- Existing mortgage balance
- Target ADU budget
- Credit score band
- DTI estimate
- Expected monthly rent (if rental use)
