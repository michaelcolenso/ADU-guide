# Appendix D — Resource Directory

These are the primary sources used in this guide and the authoritative references worth bookmarking for your own research. Every resource listed here has been evaluated for reliability and currency. Where a resource requires periodic re-verification (legislation, fee schedules, program availability), that's noted.

Organized by category. Use the section headers to navigate to what you need.

---

## ADU Law and Policy — National and Multi-State

**Pew Charitable Trusts — Housing Policy Research**
https://www.pewtrusts.org/en/research-and-analysis/topics/housing

Pew tracks ADU legislation across states with rigorous sourcing and regular updates. Their September 2025 analysis of state ADU laws is the most comprehensive national survey available. Start here for a current count of which states have enacted ADU reform and what those reforms require of local governments. Published without commercial interest.

**Sightline Institute — ADU Policy Tracker**
https://www.sightline.org/research/housing/

Seattle-based think tank covering housing policy across the Pacific Northwest and beyond. Sightline publishes detailed analyses of state ADU legislation — particularly strong on Washington, Oregon, and California — with plain-language explanation of what bills do and don't require. Useful for understanding how state law interacts with local ordinances.

**Accessory Dwellings (accessorydwellings.org)**
https://www.accessorydwellings.org

One of the longest-running independent ADU information sites, run by Kol Peterson, an ADU advocate and consultant based in Portland. Strong on Oregon policy and the practical experience of building ADUs. The site includes case studies, policy commentary, and updated regulatory summaries. Note: Kol Peterson provides ADU consulting services; read the policy content independently from any commercial material.

**National Conference of State Legislatures — Housing Database**
https://www.ncsl.org/research/human-services/state-laws-and-legislation-related-to-housing.aspx

NCSL maintains a searchable database of state legislation on housing topics including ADUs. Useful for verifying whether a specific bill has passed and finding the official bill text. Less interpretive than Pew or Sightline but more authoritative as a primary source index.

## State ADU Law — Primary Sources

The most reliable source for your state's ADU requirements is the state's own legislative or regulatory body. These are the primary source URLs for the five Foundation tier states and several others with active ADU programs. For a complete state-by-state reference, see Chapter 5.

**California — HCD ADU Handbook and Statute Reference**
https://www.hcd.ca.gov/policy-and-research/accessory-dwelling-units

The California Department of Housing and Community Development publishes an ADU handbook that translates state law into plain language for homeowners and local governments. Updated regularly as legislation changes. Also includes a lookup tool to check whether a local jurisdiction's ADU ordinance complies with state law — use this if your city's ADU ordinance seems more restrictive than what California statutes allow.

**Washington — Department of Commerce ADU Overview**
https://www.commerce.wa.gov/growth-management/housing-planning/adus/

The Washington Department of Commerce publishes a summary of HB 1337 requirements and guidance for local jurisdictions. Includes a list of planning documents and code language local governments must update. Useful for understanding what Washington state requires and verifying whether your city is in compliance.

**Oregon — DLCD Housing Resources**
https://www.oregon.gov/lcd/NH/Pages/Housing.aspx

The Oregon Department of Land Conservation and Development covers ADU requirements under HB 2001 and related statutes. Oregon's statewide land use planning system means that state housing law has direct authority over local zoning in ways that differ from most other states.

**Massachusetts — Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities**
https://www.mass.gov/orgs/executive-office-of-housing-and-livable-communities

Massachusetts enacted broad ADU reform under the Affordable Homes Act. The Executive Office publishes guidance for homeowners and municipalities. Check here for the most current interpretation of what by-right ADU approval means in Massachusetts and whether your city or town has complied.

**Colorado — DOLA Housing Resources**
https://dola.colorado.gov/housing/

The Colorado Department of Local Affairs covers the state's 2024 HB 1175 requirements, which mandate ADU allowances in certain jurisdictions. Includes guidance documents and a list of affected municipalities.

## Federal Mortgage and Financing Resources

**Fannie Mae — ADU Lending Guidelines**
https://www.fanniemae.com/research-and-insights/topics/accessory-dwelling-units

Fannie Mae has published specific guidelines for ADU lending, including how ADU rental income can be used in mortgage qualification and how ADU value is treated in appraisals. If you are financing or refinancing through a conventional lender using Fannie Mae's secondary market standards, these guidelines govern how your transaction will be underwritten. Useful for understanding what lenders must do, which gives you leverage in the conversation.

**Freddie Mac — ADU Guidance**
https://sf.freddiemac.com/articles/insights/accessory-dwelling-units

Freddie Mac's guidance on ADU appraisal and lending. Covers the treatment of ADU rental income in qualifying calculations and the comparable sales methodology required when appraising properties with ADUs. Less frequently updated than Fannie Mae's materials but worth cross-referencing.

**FHA — 203(k) Rehabilitation Mortgage Program**
https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/sfh/203k

HUD's overview of the FHA 203(k) program, which allows financing of renovation costs (including ADU construction on an existing structure) into a purchase or refinance mortgage. Limited to owner-occupied properties and subject to FHA loan limits. Useful if you are purchasing a property and want to finance ADU construction simultaneously.

**Consumer Financial Protection Bureau — Home Equity Loans and HELOCs**
https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-home-equity-loan-en-106/

The CFPB publishes plain-language consumer guides on home equity financing products — useful as a baseline reference before you talk to lenders. The CFPB site also allows you to submit complaints about lender behavior if something goes wrong.

## Construction Cost Data

**NAHB (National Association of Home Builders) — Cost of Construction Survey**
https://www.nahb.org/news-and-economics/housing-economics/surveys/cost-of-construction-survey

NAHB publishes annual cost-of-construction surveys based on builder-reported data. The survey covers single-family construction costs by region and is the most widely cited primary source for residential construction cost benchmarks. Useful for verifying that the cost ranges in Chapter 10 are consistent with current market data in your region.

**RSMeans Cost Data**
https://www.rsmeans.com

RSMeans (now part of Gordian) publishes the construction industry's most detailed cost databases — used by estimators, project managers, and owners' representatives to verify contractor pricing. The online tools require a subscription, but the published annual cost books are available at many public and university libraries. If you're trying to evaluate whether a contractor's unit prices are reasonable, RSMeans is the most authoritative reference.

**Angi (formerly Angie's List) — ADU Cost Guide**
https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-do-adu-costs.htm

Angi aggregates consumer-reported project costs and publishes category guides. The ADU cost guide is updated periodically and provides ballpark cost ranges by ADU type. Treat Angi data as a directional reference, not a bid target — consumer-reported costs vary widely based on project scope and location. Use it to sanity-check early estimates, not to validate final bids.

## Aging in Place and Multigenerational Design

**AARP — Livable Communities ADU Resources**
https://www.aarp.org/livable-communities/housing/info-2019/accessory-dwelling-units-adu.html

AARP has been one of the most active national advocates for ADU policy reform, framing ADUs as a solution for multigenerational housing and aging-in-place. Their policy resources include state-by-state summaries and model ordinance language. The AARP Livable Communities program also publishes practical design guidance for ADUs intended to house aging family members.

**National Aging in Place Council**
https://www.ageinplace.org

The NAIPC connects homeowners with certified aging-in-place specialists — architects, contractors, and occupational therapists who specialize in designing and building for accessibility and long-term livability. If your ADU is intended for a family member with current or anticipated mobility limitations, an aging-in-place specialist is worth consulting during design development.

**HUD — Fair Housing Act Design Manual**
https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_design_manual

If your ADU will be rented (as opposed to used only for family), certain accessibility requirements may apply under the Fair Housing Act. The Design Manual explains which buildings are covered and what features are required. Most single-family ADUs on private lots are not subject to Fair Housing Act accessibility requirements, but if you're converting a structure in a multifamily context or renting to the public, verify your obligations before design is finalized.

## Zoning and Land Use

**American Planning Association — Zoning Practice**
https://www.planning.org/zoning/

APA publishes research on zoning best practices including housing density, ADU policy, and form-based codes. Their Zoning Practice newsletter covers current reforms in accessible language. Useful for understanding how planning professionals think about ADU regulation — which helps you navigate conversations with local planning departments.

**Planetizen — Housing Policy News**
https://www.planetizen.com/topic/housing-policy

Planetizen covers urban planning and housing policy news daily. Useful for staying current on ADU legislation, court decisions affecting local zoning, and policy trends across states. The quality of individual articles varies — treat this as a news aggregator and verify claims that affect your project with primary sources.

## Short-Term Rental Regulation

**National League of Cities — Short-Term Rental Regulation Tracker**
https://www.nlc.org/resource/cities-and-short-term-rentals/

The NLC tracks how cities regulate short-term rentals (Airbnb, VRBO, and similar platforms) and publishes an overview of regulatory approaches — permits, owner-occupancy requirements, night limits, and tax collection. If you're considering using your ADU as a short-term rental, check this tracker alongside your city's current ordinance. STR regulations change frequently and the NLC tracker may lag behind local ordinances; always verify with your city's planning or business license department.

## Tenant-Landlord Law

**HUD — Renter's Rights and Landlord Responsibilities**
https://www.hud.gov/topics/rental_assistance/tenantrights

HUD provides a state-by-state guide to tenant rights and landlord obligations covering fair housing, security deposits, habitability standards, and notice requirements. Before you rent your ADU, understand your legal obligations as a landlord in your state. The HUD resource is a starting point; your state attorney general's office often publishes more detailed landlord-tenant guides specific to your jurisdiction.

**Nolo — Landlord and Tenant Law**
https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/landlord-tenant

Nolo publishes plain-language legal guides for landlords, organized by state. The content covers lease requirements, security deposit rules, habitability standards, and eviction procedures. Nolo's materials are written by attorneys and reviewed regularly but are not a substitute for legal advice on specific situations. Use them for orientation; consult a local landlord-tenant attorney for anything involving a potential dispute.

## Building Codes

**International Code Council — I-Codes**
https://www.iccsafe.org/codes-tech-support/codes/

The ICC publishes the International Residential Code (IRC) and International Building Code (IBC), which most states adopt (with amendments) as their base building code. The current edition is 2024, though many jurisdictions are still on 2021 or 2018 editions. Understanding which code cycle your jurisdiction is on helps you evaluate design and specification decisions. The ICC allows free online access to the most recent I-Codes for reference.

## Recommended Reading

**The Owner-Builder Book** by Mark Smith and Elaine Smith
A practical guide for homeowners managing their own construction projects. Covers contractor selection, contracts, scheduling, and cost control. Written for a general residential audience rather than ADU specifically, but the management principles translate directly. Recommended reading before you enter contractor negotiations.

**Accessory Apartment Programs: A Guide for Local Officials** (HUD Office of Policy Development and Research)
https://www.huduser.gov/portal/publications/affhsg/accapart.html

An older HUD publication but still relevant for understanding how local governments think about ADU programs and what regulatory mechanisms they use. Useful if you're trying to understand why your local planning department makes the decisions it makes.
