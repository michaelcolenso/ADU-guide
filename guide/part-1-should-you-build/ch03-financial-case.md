# Chapter 3: The Financial Case - ROI, Rental Income, and Property Value

You just got three ADU bids. One is low enough to feel suspicious, one looks realistic, and one is high enough to make you wonder if this project still works. This is the chapter that turns that moment into a decision framework.

## Build Your Rent Case From Conservative Inputs

Start with a conservative rent floor, not your best-case Zillow search. HUD Fair Market Rent (FMR) data is a practical baseline because it is standardized and updated annually for metro areas [S1].

For FY 2026 FMR schedules, example 1-bedroom baselines include roughly $2,085 in the Los Angeles HMFA, $2,146 in Seattle-Bellevue HMFA, $1,677 in Portland metro, $1,583 in Phoenix metro, $1,562 in Austin metro, and $2,476 in Boston-Cambridge-Quincy HMFA [S2].

FMR is not your exact market rent. It is your stress-test baseline. If your project only works with rents far above local FMR bands, your risk profile is high.

## Property Value Uplift: Strong Signal, But Do Not Overgeneralize

One of the most cited data points comes from FHFA's California appraisal dataset: from 2013 to 2023, median appraised value growth was 9.34% annually for properties with ADUs versus 7.65% for properties without ADUs [S3].

That does not mean every ADU creates that exact spread in every market. It does mean appraisers and buyers are consistently pricing utility and flexibility into at least one large, data-rich market. Treat this as directional evidence, not a guaranteed multiplier.

## Quantify Multigenerational Value Like Cash Flow

Some ADUs are built for rent. Others are built for family stability. Both have financial value.

If your ADU houses a parent, adult child, or caregiver, assign a monthly replacement-cost value to that use case and include it in your model. If you skip this step, your spreadsheet systematically understates project value.

The key is to quantify the alternative cost you are avoiding, then run the same break-even logic you would use for rental income.

## Tax Mechanics You Need to Model Early

For rental-use ADUs, IRS Publication 527 confirms residential rental structures are generally depreciated over 27.5 years under MACRS/GDS [S4]. That can materially improve after-tax cash flow.

For eventual sale, IRS Publication 523 makes clear that gain attributable to depreciation is generally not excluded under the primary-residence exclusion rules and may be subject to recapture treatment [S5]. If you ignore this early, your exit math can be wrong by a meaningful amount.

You do not need to become a tax professional. You do need a tax-informed pro forma before you lock financing.

## A Practical Break-Even Framework

Use this sequence with conservative assumptions:

1. Set total project cost = hard costs + soft costs + financing costs + contingency.
2. Set net annual income = conservative annual rent - vacancy allowance - annual operating costs.
3. Set annual tax benefit = depreciation effect + deductible expenses (if applicable).
4. Set annual net benefit = net annual income + annual tax benefit + quantified family-use value (if no rent).
5. Break-even years = total project cost / annual net benefit.

Then rerun with downside assumptions: +10% total cost, -10% rent, and longer vacancy. If the project still works, your decision is likely durable.

## What a Durable ADU Deal Looks Like

A durable ADU project survives conservative rent assumptions, carries a real contingency reserve, and does not depend on flawless permitting or utility outcomes. A fragile project needs best-case rents, zero surprises, and perfect financing timing.
