# About the Author

Michael Colenso has spent more than twenty years managing construction projects across the Pacific Northwest, from a $12 million historic hotel renovation in Portland to a $200 million mixed-use development on the Seattle waterfront. He has worked in multifamily residential, commercial, hospitality, senior living, and institutional construction — always on the owner's side of the table, never as a contractor, never as a lender, never selling anything but straight advice.

His career has run almost entirely through the owner's representative role: the person hired to protect the client's interests against a room full of professionals who, however competent, have their own interests to manage. Contractors want scope certainty and profitable change orders. Architects want design freedom and creative latitude. Lenders want collateral and fees. Colenso's job, for two decades, has been to understand what all of those interests look like in practice and make sure the person writing the checks doesn't get managed by the people cashing them.

He did not set out to write a guide for homeowners building ADUs. He started getting calls from neighbors, colleagues, and friends who were navigating ADU projects and finding that the available information was either promotional, incomplete, or written by people with a financial stake in a particular outcome. One friend signed a construction contract without a draw schedule. Another hired an architect who had never pulled a permit in their city. A third used a HELOC to fund a detached ADU and learned partway through construction that her city's discretionary review process was going to add seven months to the timeline — and thousands of dollars in carrying costs she hadn't budgeted for. In each case, the mistakes were preventable. The information that would have prevented them existed. It just wasn't organized in a way a homeowner could use.

This guide is that organization. It covers the full ADU lifecycle from first feasibility check through tenant move-in, drawing on the frameworks, checklists, and hard-earned lessons from twenty years of managing projects where the stakes were real and the margin for error was narrow. The construction management principles that protect a $40 million multifamily owner from contractor overreach work at the $180,000 ADU scale too. The fundamentals — verify before you commit, put everything in writing, manage the draw schedule, don't skip inspections, budget the contingency before you define the scope — are the same at any project size. They're just not usually written down in a form that a homeowner who has never managed construction can use.

Colenso is Procore and Buildertrend certified. He has managed projects across residential, commercial, hospitality, and senior living sectors in Washington, Oregon, and northern California. He has no financial relationship with any contractor, lender, architect, prefab manufacturer, or ADU platform referenced in this guide. Every product recommendation and professional in this guide was evaluated independently.

He lives in the Pacific Northwest with his wife and two teenage children, in a house with a detached ADU he built himself. That project came in on budget and on schedule, mostly because of everything he knew from twenty years of watching other people's projects come in over both.
