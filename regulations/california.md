# California — ADU Regulation Reference

> **Last verified:** 2026-02-26
> **Tier availability:** Foundation (top 5)
> **Status:** Active legislation

---

## Enabling Legislation

| Bill | Effective Date | Summary |
|------|---------------|---------|
| AB 68 + SB 13 | 2020-01-01 | Major statewide ADU streamlining and ministerial approval framework. |
| AB 2221 + SB 897 | 2023-01-01 | Expanded/clarified state ADU standards, including siting and ministerial pathways. |
| AB 976 | 2024-01-01 | Eliminated local owner-occupancy requirements for ADUs. |
| AB 1033 | 2024-01-01 | Allows local ordinances to permit separate sale/conveyance of ADUs as condominiums. |
| SB 1211 | 2025-01-01 | Increased detached ADU allowance on lots with existing multifamily housing (up to 8, capped by existing unit count). |

**Legislative history:** California moved from local-option ADUs to a strongly preemptive statewide framework between 2017 and 2025. The core statute was recodified in Government Code Chapter 13 (Sections 66310-66342). Post-2019 bills limited discretionary review, constrained local development standards, and expanded multifamily-lot ADU pathways.

## ADU Types Permitted

| Type | Permitted? | Size Limit | Notes |
|------|-----------|------------|-------|
| Detached ADU | Yes | Up to 1,200 sq ft | State law also protects certain smaller ministerial pathways. |
| Attached ADU | Yes | Up to 50% of existing primary dwelling | Local rules cannot block protected ministerial cases. |
| Garage conversion | Yes | Existing envelope; expansion allowances apply | No replacement parking required when parking is removed/converted for ADU work. |
| JADU (internal) | Yes | 500 sq ft max | Limited to single-family context; separate JADU rules apply. |
| Multiple ADUs per lot | Yes | Multifamily: up to 8 detached ADUs (existing MF), capped by existing unit count; plus conversion ADUs | Single-family lots can typically do one ADU plus one JADU by ministerial route. |

## Dimensional and Siting Requirements

**Maximum ADU size:** Detached ADU up to 1,200 sq ft; attached ADU up to 50% of existing primary dwelling (state baseline).
**Minimum lot size:** None at state baseline for ADU approval pathways.
**Maximum height:** Under Gov. Code § 66321(4) (effective January 1, 2026), local height limits must allow at least 16 feet for detached ADUs, 18 feet in transit/multistory contexts (plus up to 2 additional feet for aligned roof pitch near transit), and up to 25 feet (or local primary-dwelling limit, whichever is lower) for attached ADUs.
**Setback requirements:**
- Front: Local objective standards may apply
- Side: No more than 4 feet for applicable new-construction ministerial pathways
- Rear: No more than 4 feet for applicable new-construction ministerial pathways
- Special provisions: No setback required for qualifying conversions in the same location/dimensions as existing structures.

**Lot coverage:** Deferred to local objective standards, subject to state preemption limits.
**Floor area ratio (FAR):** Local objective standards may apply, but cannot conflict with state ADU protections.

---

## Parking

**Parking required:** Conditional (capped).
**Exemptions:** State law limits parking demands and includes specific exemption categories (e.g., certain transit- and conversion-related scenarios under statute).
**Replacement parking:** Not required when garage/carport/covered or uncovered spaces are demolished or converted in conjunction with ADU development.

---

## Owner-Occupancy

**Owner-occupancy required:** No (for ADUs).
**Details:** Local agencies are prohibited from imposing owner-occupancy requirements on ADUs.
**Recent changes:** AB 976 removed prior owner-occupancy allowance for local governments.

---

## Fees and Costs

**Impact fees:** Gov. Code § 66311.5(c)(1) bars impact fees for ADUs of 750 square feet or less (and JADUs of 500 square feet or less); above that size, fees must be proportional.
**Permit fees:** No statewide flat cap; local fee schedules apply.
**Utility connection fees:** For many conversion/internal ADU pathways, agencies cannot require a new/separate utility connection fee; where allowed, connection/capacity charges must be proportionate.
**School fees:** Gov. Code § 66311.5(c)(3) ties ADU treatment to Education Code § 17620; ADUs/JADUs under 500 square feet are treated as construction that does not increase assessable space by 500 square feet for school-fee purposes.

---

## Utility Requirements

**Separate utility meters:** Not universally required; state law limits when separate new utility connections may be imposed.
**Sewer/septic:** Local health officer approval may be required for onsite wastewater systems.
**Electrical panel:** Building/electrical code compliance required; panel upgrades are project-specific.
**Water:** Local utility standards apply; connection charges must be proportionate where authorized.

---

## Pre-Approved Plans

**State-level program:** No single statewide pre-approved ADU plan library identified.
**Notable local programs:** Los Angeles LADBS Standard Plan Program; several California cities maintain local permit-ready plans.
**Cost/time savings:** Local programs generally reduce plan check time; city-specific timelines vary.

---

## Local Override Provisions

**Can cities restrict beyond state law?** Limited.
**Common local additions:** Objective design standards, local processing checklists, utility/sewer technical requirements.
**Preemption details:** Local agencies must process qualifying ADUs ministerially and cannot apply conflicting local standards that state law disallows.

---

## Key City-Level Variations

### Los Angeles
- LADBS runs an ADU Standard Plan Program to streamline repeatable ADU designs.
- Local implementation aligns with state rules on transit-related parking relief and no replacement parking for garage conversion.
- Local agencies must decide qualifying detached ADU applications within 30 days when using qualifying preapproved/previously approved plans under Gov. Code § 65852.27(b).

### San Diego
- City adopted ADU/JADU amendments effective August 22, 2025 (outside Coastal Zone), including updates tied to state-law alignment.
- City materials reflect multifamily detached ADU allowances up to 8 where allowed by state framework.
- Local bonus/incentive program details have changed repeatedly; verify parcel-specific applicability.

## Key Takeaways for Homeowners

- California remains one of the most preemptive ADU states: ministerial review is the default for qualifying projects.
- Detached ADUs can reach 1,200 sq ft statewide, and multifamily lots now have larger detached ADU potential.
- Owner-occupancy is not a barrier for ADUs, but JADU and local short-term-rental rules still matter.
- Parking rules are heavily constrained by state law; replacement parking generally cannot be forced for ADU conversions.
- Always confirm city implementation memos before design finalization, especially in coastal zones and fire overlays.
