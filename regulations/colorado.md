# Colorado — ADU Regulation Reference

> **Last verified:** 2026-02-26
> **Tier availability:** Foundation (top 5)
> **Status:** Active legislation

---

## Enabling Legislation

| Bill | Effective Date | Summary |
|------|---------------|---------|
| HB24-1152 | 2024-05-13 | Creates statewide ADU framework for "subject jurisdictions," requiring one ADU by administrative approval where single-unit detached homes are allowed (on/after 2025-06-30). |
| HB24-1007 (related housing framework) | 2024-07-01 | Broad occupancy-limit reform that interacts with local housing regulation context. |
| HB24-1304 (related parking framework) | 2024-07-01 | Parking-minimum reform context relevant to local ADU implementation. |

**Legislative history:** Colorado moved from local-option ADUs to a state-mandated baseline in metro-area "subject jurisdictions" via HB24-1152. The law also addresses PUD/HOA constraints in supportive-jurisdiction context and funds implementation support.

## ADU Types Permitted

| Type | Permitted? | Size Limit | Notes |
|------|-----------|------------|-------|
| Detached ADU | Yes | Must include state-required size band (500-750 sq ft) in subject jurisdictions | Localities may allow larger units. |
| Attached ADU | Yes | Must include state-required size band (500-750 sq ft) in subject jurisdictions | Includes additions/internal reconfiguration. |
| Garage conversion | Yes | Subject to building and local objective standards | Treated as ADU conversion pathway. |
| JADU (internal) | No (state term not used) | N/A | Colorado framework uses ADU terminology. |
| Multiple ADUs per lot | Baseline 1 required | One ADU minimum right in subject jurisdictions | Localities may allow more than one. |

## Dimensional and Siting Requirements

**Maximum ADU size:** Subject jurisdictions must permit ADUs in a size range that includes 500-750 sq ft; localities can allow larger (and/or smaller) units.
**Minimum lot size:** Cannot be more restrictive than lot standards for single-unit detached homes in the same zone.
**Maximum height:** HB24-1152 does not set one statewide numeric ADU height floor; it requires subject jurisdictions to avoid ADU-specific dimensional standards that are more restrictive than allowed by statute.
**Setback requirements:**
- Front: Local code applies
- Side: Cannot exceed side setback standards for the primary single-unit detached dwelling in applicable zones
- Rear: Cannot exceed standards for other accessory buildings or 5 feet, whichever is greater
- Special provisions: Restrictive ADU-specific design/dimensional standards are prohibited in subject jurisdictions.

**Lot coverage:** ADU standards cannot be more restrictive than primary single-unit detached baseline where prohibited by HB24-1152.
**Floor area ratio (FAR):** Local FAR may apply if not used as a prohibited restrictive standard.

---

## Parking

**Parking required:** Conditional and limited.
**Exemptions:** Subject jurisdictions generally cannot automatically require new off-street parking for ADUs.
**Replacement parking:** HB24-1152 does not create a universal statewide ADU replacement-parking mandate; local treatment varies within the bill's parking-limit framework.

---

## Owner-Occupancy

**Owner-occupancy required:** Generally no.
**Details:** Subject-jurisdiction laws cannot require long-term owner occupancy of ADU/primary unit; limited exceptions exist at permit/STR stages.
**Recent changes:** HB24-1152 preempts many local owner-occupancy restrictions.

---

## Fees and Costs

**Impact fees:** Generally applicable impact fees may still be charged.
**Permit fees:** Local permit and review fees apply.
**Utility connection fees:** Local governments can require utility/service capacity evidence and code compliance.
**School fees:** Colorado ADU statute does not create a separate statewide ADU school-fee schedule.

---

## Utility Requirements

**Separate utility meters:** Local utility policy and code requirements govern.
**Sewer/septic:** Jurisdictions may require proof of wastewater treatment capacity.
**Electrical panel:** Project-specific electrical code compliance required.
**Water:** Jurisdictions/utilities may require proof of water supply capacity.

---

## Pre-Approved Plans

**State-level program:** State law funds/authorizes support for local pre-approved ADU plan efforts through DOLA programs.
**Notable local programs:** Denver and Boulder publish local ADU guidance; local permit-ready plan ecosystems are expanding.
**Cost/time savings:** Local pre-approved plans and streamlined review can reduce soft costs and permitting duration.

---

## Local Override Provisions

**Can cities restrict beyond state law?** Limited in subject jurisdictions.
**Common local additions:** Objective code checks, utility capacity documentation, historic district review.
**Preemption details:** Subject jurisdictions must allow one ADU by administrative process where single-unit detached housing is allowed; PUD and HOA restrictions are constrained under state law.

---

## Key City-Level Variations

### Denver
- Denver approved citywide ADU zoning expansion in November 2024, effective December 16, 2024.
- City permit materials state one ADU per zone lot and require professional permitting pathways.
- Utility/addressing and zoning review requirements remain detailed and site-specific.

### Boulder
- Boulder approved ADU code updates in February 2025, effective March 8, 2025.
- City states owner-occupancy is no longer required as of March 8, 2025.
- Boulder continues local standards for zone eligibility, historic areas, and short-term-rental interactions.

## Key Takeaways for Homeowners

- In Colorado subject jurisdictions, one ADU is now a state-guaranteed baseline in single-unit detached areas.
- Local governments have less room to use ADU-only dimensional/occupancy barriers.
- Utility and life-safety code compliance still drives cost and schedule.
- Denver and Boulder already have robust local ADU materials that should be checked early.
- If you are outside a subject jurisdiction, local code may still be more variable.
