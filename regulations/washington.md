# Washington — ADU Regulation Reference

> **Last verified:** 2026-02-26
> **Tier availability:** Foundation (top 5)
> **Status:** Active legislation

---

## Enabling Legislation

| Bill | Effective Date | Summary |
|------|---------------|---------|
| HB 1337 (2023) | 2023-07-23 (implementation deadlines begin 2024-06-30 by jurisdiction size) | Requires fully planning GMA jurisdictions to allow two ADUs per residential lot in applicable urban growth areas, with statewide preemption of conflicting local rules after deadlines. |
| SHB 1353 (2025) | 2025-07-27 | Authorizes optional city self-certification programs for detached ADU permit applications. |

**Legislative history:** Washington shifted from piecemeal local ADU rules to statewide minimums through HB 1337. Implementation is tied to Growth Management Act periodic update deadlines, with state preemption for conflicting local provisions in noncompliant jurisdictions.

## ADU Types Permitted

| Type | Permitted? | Size Limit | Notes |
|------|-----------|------------|-------|
| Detached ADU | Yes | Local max cannot be below 1,000 sq ft | State minimum right-to-build standard. |
| Attached ADU | Yes | Local max cannot be below 1,000 sq ft | Can be within/attached to principal structure. |
| Garage conversion | Yes | Subject to local code/building standards | Counts toward ADU allowance where compliant. |
| JADU (internal) | No (state term not used) | N/A | Washington statute uses ADU/attached/detached definitions. |
| Multiple ADUs per lot | Yes | At least 2 ADUs per residential lot in covered areas | Attached, detached, or combination. |

## Dimensional and Siting Requirements

**Maximum ADU size:** Jurisdictions must allow at least 1,000 sq ft.
**Minimum lot size:** Under RCW 36.70A.681(1)(a)(iii), fully planning jurisdictions may not require a minimum lot size for single-family housing greater than 3,200 square feet in zones where ADUs are allowed.
**Maximum height:** Under RCW 36.70A.681(1)(a)(vi), jurisdictions may not set an ADU height limit below 24 feet unless the principal dwelling in that zone is also capped below 24 feet.
**Setback requirements:**
- Front: Local objective standards allowed within state limits
- Side: No statewide numeric side-yard floor in RCW 36.70A.681; local setbacks are allowed but cannot conflict with statewide ADU minimum allowances
- Rear: No statewide numeric rear-yard floor in RCW 36.70A.681; local setbacks are allowed but cannot conflict with statewide ADU minimum allowances
- Special provisions: Conversion pathways and objective standards apply; local regulations must remain consistent with RCW requirements.

**Lot coverage:** Local standards allowed if not in conflict with state ADU statutes.
**Floor area ratio (FAR):** Local FAR can apply but cannot negate state ADU allowances.

## Parking

**Parking required:** Conditional.
**Exemptions:** Parking requirements must be modified based on distance from major transit stop and/or lot size under state framework.
**Replacement parking:** RCW 36.70A.681 does not establish a statewide replacement-parking requirement for converted garages; local code treatment varies.

---

## Owner-Occupancy

**Owner-occupancy required:** Generally no.
**Details:** State framework prohibits owner-occupancy requirements, with limited short-term-rental-related exception pathways.
**Recent changes:** HB 1337 removed owner-occupancy as a common local ADU barrier in covered areas.

---

## Fees and Costs

**Impact fees:** Capped at no more than 50% of impact fees charged for the principal unit.
**Permit fees:** Local permit and review fees still apply.
**Utility connection fees:** Local jurisdictions set utility requirements; state guidance encourages proportional standards.
**School fees:** RCW 36.70A.681 includes ADU impact-fee limits but does not create a separate statewide ADU school-fee schedule.

---

## Utility Requirements

**Separate utility meters:** Local option; many jurisdictions allow shared connections subject to utility review.
**Sewer/septic:** Site-specific; local health/sewer authority requirements apply.
**Electrical panel:** Project-specific electrical code compliance required.
**Water:** Jurisdiction/utility-specific connection and capacity rules apply.

---

## Pre-Approved Plans

**State-level program:** No single mandatory state plan catalog, but Commerce publishes examples and implementation resources.
**Notable local programs:** Seattle ADUniverse; Kirkland/Lacey/Leavenworth/Renton permit-ready plan programs.
**Cost/time savings:** Seattle indicates pre-approved DADU permit timelines can drop to roughly 2-6 weeks in many cases.

---

## Local Override Provisions

**Can cities restrict beyond state law?** Limited.
**Common local additions:** Objective design standards, utility/sewer requirements, tree/environmental review overlays.
**Preemption details:** If a jurisdiction misses GMA periodic update deadlines, conflicting ADU rules are preempted by state statute.

---

## Key City-Level Variations

### Seattle
- Allows ADUs under Seattle Municipal Code with no off-street parking requirement and no owner-occupancy requirement.
- Seattle ADUniverse provides pre-approved DADU plans; city materials cite 2-6 week permit timelines for standard-plan paths.
- Local sewer capacity charge process still applies.

### Tacoma
- City materials indicate up to two attached and/or detached ADUs on legally established lots.
- 2025 tip sheets reference up to 1,000 sq ft ADU size in Urban Residential context.
- Tacoma offers pre-approved DADU plan options.

---

## Key Takeaways for Homeowners

- In covered Washington jurisdictions, two ADUs per lot is now a statewide baseline.
- Local governments cannot use owner-occupancy and full impact-fee treatment as default barriers.
- Parking mandates are constrained, especially near major transit.
- City implementation timing still matters because GMA periodic update deadlines control local code transitions.
- Pre-approved plans can materially reduce permitting time in several cities.
