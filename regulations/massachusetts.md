# Massachusetts — ADU Regulation Reference

> **Last verified:** 2026-02-26
> **Tier availability:** Foundation (top 5)
> **Status:** Active legislation

## Enabling Legislation

| Bill | Effective Date | Summary |
|------|---------------|---------|
| Chapter 150 of the Acts of 2024 (Affordable Homes Act), Section 8 | 2025-02-02 | Legalized by-right "Protected Use" ADUs in single-family zoning districts under statewide standards. |
| M.G.L. c. 40A, § 3 | Current | Statutory by-right ADU framework (size, by-right process, key local limits). |
| 760 CMR 71.00 | 2025-01-31 | State regulation implementing Protected Use ADU administration details. |

**Legislative history:** Massachusetts moved from local-by-local ADU adoption to statewide by-right legalization through the 2024 Affordable Homes Act. HLC rulemaking (760 CMR 71.00) followed, with local conflicting rules becoming unenforceable for qualifying Protected Use ADUs as of early 2025.

## Dimensional and Siting Requirements

**Maximum ADU size:** Protected Use ADU must not exceed 900 sq ft or one-half of the principal dwelling gross floor area, whichever is smaller.
**Minimum lot size:** State law bars unreasonable restrictions that conflict with Protected Use ADU rights, but it does not set one statewide numeric lot-size minimum.
**Maximum height:** No single statewide ADU height number; local dimensional standards apply if consistent with law.
**Setback requirements:**
- Front: Local dimensional rules may apply if not in conflict with Protected Use ADU standards
- Side: Local dimensional rules may apply if not in conflict
- Rear: Local dimensional rules may apply if not in conflict
- Special provisions: Special permit/discretionary zoning approval is prohibited for Protected Use ADUs.

**Lot coverage:** Local zoning rules may apply where not inconsistent with c.40A §3 and 760 CMR 71.
**Floor area ratio (FAR):** Local zoning rules may apply where not inconsistent with c.40A §3 and 760 CMR 71.

## Parking

**Parking required:** Conditional.
**Exemptions:** For Protected Use ADUs, no additional parking may be required if the lot is within one-half mile walking distance of a commuter rail station, subway station, ferry terminal, or bus station.
**Replacement parking:** No statewide ADU-specific replacement-parking requirement is created in c.40A §3; local parking replacement treatment depends on municipal zoning/building rules where consistent with state law.

---

## Owner-Occupancy

**Owner-occupancy required:** No (for Protected Use ADUs).
**Details:** State law/regulation bars owner-occupancy requirements for qualifying Protected Use ADUs.
**Recent changes:** This is a major shift from many prior local ADU ordinances.

---

## Fees and Costs

**Impact fees:** Massachusetts ADU statute does not create a statewide ADU-specific impact-fee cap; local fee treatment follows general municipal fee authority and code processes.
**Permit fees:** Local building permit and inspection fee schedules apply.
**Utility connection fees:** Local utility district/municipal standards apply.
**School fees:** Massachusetts ADU statute does not establish a separate statewide ADU school-fee schedule.

---

## Utility Requirements

**Separate utility meters:** Not mandated statewide; local utility/building code practices govern.
**Sewer/septic:** Local board of health and sewer authority requirements apply.
**Electrical panel:** Code-driven; upgrade needs are project-specific.
**Water:** Local utility connection/capacity requirements apply.

## Pre-Approved Plans

**State-level program:** No single statewide pre-approved plan library identified.
**Notable local programs:** Boston provides ADU guide/program resources; local municipal initiatives vary.
**Cost/time savings:** Primarily local-process dependent.

## Local Override Provisions

**Can cities restrict beyond state law?** Limited for Protected Use ADUs.
**Common local additions:** Objective dimensional standards, health/safety code requirements, utility/sewer review.
**Preemption details:** Inconsistent local zoning rules are unenforceable for qualifying Protected Use ADUs; applicants can proceed through building permit channels.

---

## Key City-Level Variations

### Boston
- Boston is not governed by the same zoning enabling structure as most municipalities under c.40A and uses its own zoning/permitting pathways.
- City ADU resources describe owner-occupied 1-3 family homeowner pathways and local program support.
- Some projects may still require ZBA or additional local review depending on zoning context.

### Worcester
- Worcester adopted ADU zoning before statewide by-right law and continues updating local zoning documents.
- City ADU guidance includes local dimensional/process details that homeowners must reconcile with statewide Protected Use ADU rights.
- Worcester zoning amendments continued into 2026, so current local text should be checked case-by-case.

## Key Takeaways for Homeowners

- As of February 2, 2025, qualifying ADUs are by-right statewide in single-family zones under MA law (with Boston carveout considerations).
- Protected Use ADUs are capped at 900 sq ft (or half the principal unit, whichever is smaller).
- Owner-occupancy is not a valid barrier for Protected Use ADUs.
- Local health/building/utility compliance still governs project delivery and timing.
- Boston and some preexisting local ordinances require extra care to confirm which rules apply.
